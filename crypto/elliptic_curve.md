# Things to look at for elliptic curve

The curve should be well known.

You can look at https://safecurves.cr.yp.to/ for specs of each curve, and see what are the problems related to one curve. 

The order of the generator must be a prime of at least 224 bits.

The number of points on the curve must be different of p (we call that an anomalous curve)

It's required that $`\forall r \in \llbracket 1~;~ 10^4 \rrbracket ,  p^r \neq 1 [n]`$ (known as MOV condition)

## Points on curve

When we do a computation, it's important to verify that the points are on the curve.
If an attacker does an ECDH with points with low order (if there are low factors in order(P)) or not on curve, the attacker will be able to leak some bits of the secret key of server in the first case, TODO second case.

## Sub group attacks

The generator should have a prime order to avoid subgroup attacks.

Example: if ord(G) = 8 * l, and P = d * G, then you can retrieve 3 bits of d by multiplying the point P with l, and compare the result with 0, lG, 2lG, ... 7lG.

## Attacks on ECDLP

ECDLP = Ellitpic Curve Discrete Logarithm Problem

### Pollard rho

This algorithm uses the birthday paradox to find a collision that will permit to solve the discrete logarithm. This algorithm could be parallelized.
It computes a progression, and the sequence computed should collide after $`\sqrt{n \pi \over 2}`$. It's implemented in sagemath.

### Pohlig-Hellman
This algorithm is efficient if there are multiple subgroups of prime order (no big factor in factorization of o(G)). So to verify this, you just have to factor the order of the generator.

Example: if o(G) = 2^2 * 3 * 5^4, Pohlig-Hellman algorithm will solve ECDLP mod 2^2, mod 3 and mod 5^4, and will use CRT to obtain the discrete log mod o(G)
